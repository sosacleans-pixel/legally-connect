About your case

Do I have a valid personal injury case?

You may have a case if you were hurt because someone else was careless. A personal injury claim generally needs to show three things:

  1. The other person had a duty to act safely, such as following traffic laws.
  2. They did not.
  3. Their mistake directly caused your injuries and your financial losses.

Legally Connect is a referral service, not a law firm. An attorney can review the facts of your situation and tell you what may apply.

Should I make a claim if I do not feel pain right away?

After a crash, your body can go into shock and release adrenaline, which can hide pain completely. Serious injuries, such as whiplash, concussions, or internal bleeding, often do not hurt until days later. See a doctor right away to protect your health. Prompt medical care also creates records that can matter later. An attorney can advise you on whether and when to make a claim.

Costs and case value

How much is my personal injury case worth?

There is no exact formula. The value of a case is often based on two things:

  • Documented expenses, such as medical bills, pharmacy receipts, lost pay, and vehicle repair bills.
  • Personal impact, including physical pain, emotional stress, and how the injury changed your daily life.

An attorney can review your records and explain what may apply in your situation.

The legal process and timelines

How long do I have to file a lawsuit?

In Utah, many personal injury lawsuits from a car accident must be filed within four years of the accident. Some situations have a shorter deadline. If the deadline that applies to you passes, you can lose the right to recover. An attorney can tell you which deadline applies to your situation.

Will I have to go to court?

Many personal injury cases are resolved without a trial. A trial may be needed if the insurance company will not offer a fair resolution. The attorney on your case would handle that process.

How long will my case take?

It often depends on your medical recovery. Simpler cases with clear fault and more minor injuries may resolve in a few months. Cases with serious injuries and longer medical care can take a year or more. It is usually unwise to settle before the full medical picture is known. An attorney can advise you on timing.

After an accident

What should I do right after an accident?
  1. Get medical care right away.
  2. Call the police and ask them to complete an official accident report.
  3. Take photos and videos of the scene, the cars, any hazards, and your injuries.
  4. Exchange contact and insurance information with everyone involved.
What should I say if the other driver's insurance company calls?

You do not have to give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company, and you should not admit fault. Adjusters may sound friendly, but their job is to protect their company. You can tell them you are working with Legally Connect and share our WhatsApp number, (385) 490-6607, then end the call.

Utah auto insurance

How does Utah's no-fault system work?

Utah requires your own auto insurance to include personal injury protection, often called PIP. That coverage generally pays at least the first $3,000 of your reasonable medical bills after a crash, no matter who caused it. Utah law generally allows a claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering when medical expenses are more than $3,000, or when certain serious injuries apply, such as a broken bone, permanent impairment, or death. An attorney can explain how these rules apply to you.

Will my insurance go up if I open a claim and I was not at fault?

Utah law (Utah Code 31A-19a-212) does not allow a personal auto insurer to raise your premium because of a motor vehicle claim in which you, or someone using your car with permission, were not at fault. That protection applies when a policy is issued or renewed. Your rate can still change for other reasons, such as a company-wide rate change. If you think your premium went up because of a crash that was not your fault, ask your insurer why.

Documents to gather

What documents should I gather?

If you have them, these items can help us and the attorney review your situation:

  1. Accident and official records
    • The police report or information-exchange form from the officer at the scene
    • The officer's name, badge number, and department if you did not get a full report
    • Any traffic citation issued to the other driver at the scene
  2. Photos and scene evidence
    • Photos or videos of the scene, including vehicle positions, skid marks, traffic signs, weather, or road hazards
    • Close-up and wide photos of damage to your vehicle, the interior, or personal property
    • Clear photos of your injuries from the day of the crash through your recovery
  3. Insurance and identification
    • Your auto insurance declarations page or a breakdown that shows your coverage, including personal injury protection (PIP)
    • The other driver's contact and insurance information
    • Your health insurance card details, which can help with medical bills while a claim is pending
  4. Medical records and financial impact
    • Emergency or urgent-care records, such as discharge papers or instructions from your first visit
    • Medical bills and receipts from hospitals, physical therapists, chiropractors, or pharmacies
    • Proof of lost income, such as recent pay stubs or calendar records of hours missed for medical visits or recovery
